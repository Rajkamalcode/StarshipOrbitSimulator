How this was worked out

  • The path is a circular 275 km orbit with the Earth turning beneath it (about 22.5 degrees of longitude per orbit) and J2 drift of the orbit plane included.
  • The ship leaves Starbase heading south-east through the Florida Straits, as earlier flights did. Checked against Flight 11: this plane passes within about 130 km of that flight's Indian Ocean splashdown point.
  • After 9 hours 40 minutes the model puts the ship over the south-east Pacific near 26 degrees south, matching SpaceX's "west of Chile" splashdown.
  • Ascent and the coast to the orbit-raising burn (dashed, before about T+40 min) and the final deorbit and reentry (hatched) are approximations, not real trajectories.
  • Pass times are model estimates and could move by a few minutes. SpaceX says Mission Control may cut the flight to two or five orbits if the ship isn't healthy.
  • The coastline is a hand-built low-resolution outline, so treat city-to-coast distances as approximate.
